# Roadmap: speaking Castilian Spanish fluently

**Status: PROPOSED, 4 Oct 2026.** This becomes our plan once we agree on the decisions in [section 8](#8-decisions-to-agree). After that, every new layer must name the step of this roadmap it covers.

## 1. Destination

In about **12 months, at about 15 minutes a day**, you speak about work and everyday life in Spain at **CEFR level B2**. Past tenses, conditional and subjunctive come out without stopping to think.

Concretely, you can:

- **Run a stand-up or a 1:1:** what you've done, what's blocked, what's next — choosing *he hecho* or *hice* correctly.
- **Explain what went wrong in a project:** in order, with background and causes (imperfecto, indefinido, pluscuamperfecto).
- **Hold your position in a meeting:** agree, disagree, propose and set conditions (conditional and subjunctive).
- **Handle the harder conversations:** give and receive feedback, negotiate, and stay polite in a difficult conversation.
- **Chat at coffee and lunch:** plans, stories, opinions and jokes, reacting naturally.

| In numbers | Target |
|---|---|
| Production cards, said out loud until instant | **~1,000** (326 today) |
| Recognition cards (understanding slang) | ~110–150 |
| Out-loud card repetitions in the year | ~7,000 |
| External check (optional) | DELE B2 speaking exam (Instituto Cervantes) |

**Why ~1,000 cards and not 5,000?**

1. It's what 15 minutes a day can learn **and keep alive** (section 7).
2. It covers the B1–B2 speaking content of the Instituto Cervantes curriculum with several examples of each structure.
3. Past that point, real conversation does more for you than more cards (section 2, Four Strands).

## 2. The method behind it

No single method answers every question, so the plan combines well-established frameworks. Each one answers one question:

| Question | Framework | What it means for us |
|---|---|---|
| Where is the finish line? | **CEFR**, Council of Europe (2001, 2020) | Its B2 "can-do" descriptors for spoken interaction and production define the destination and the checkpoints. |
| What to learn, and in what order? | **Plan Curricular del Instituto Cervantes** (PCIC, 2006) | The official reference for the Spanish of Spain, level by level: grammar, communicative functions, conversation strategies. Each layer takes its content from it. |
| In what form? | **Lexical approach / formulaic language** (Lewis 1993; Wray 2002) | Fluent speakers pull out ready-made chunks, so cards are whole sentences in context, not rules or single words. |
| How does it become automatic? | **Skill acquisition theory** (DeKeyser 2007) | Only practising the real skill makes it fast. So you say it out loud, and press Known only when it's instant. |
| How do you keep it? | **Spaced retrieval** (Leitner 1972; Cepeda et al. 2006) | Review at growing gaps. The app's ↻ does this: 1 → 3 → 7 → 14 → 30 → 60 → 120 days. |
| How do you balance your time? | **Four Strands** (Nation 2007) | Cards cover two of the four strands (language study and fluency). Real conversation at work and listening to Spain Spanish cover the other two. |
| How do we find what's missing? | **Output hypothesis** (Swain 1995) | Speaking shows you what you can't say yet. The weekly gap log turns those moments into cards. |

## 3. Where we are (October 2026)

- **438 cards:** 326 production (P-A1–P-A4, W-1, W-2) and 112 recognition (Coloquial 1–3).
- **Covered:** indefinido (irregular and regular), imperfecto vs indefinido, future and conditional, si-clauses. W-1 and W-2 start the perfecto, the subjunctive and office conversation.
- **Your bottleneck:** choosing and conjugating tenses in real time. That means the past tenses, the perfecto, the conditional and the subjunctive.

## 4. The route: 16 layers in 4 phases

A **layer** is 50 cards: 5 groups of 10, one group per sitting.

The circled numbers are the six function groups of the Cervantes curriculum (PCIC) that a layer trains:

1. ① giving and asking for information
2. ② opinions and attitudes
3. ③ wishes and feelings
4. ④ influencing others (requests, advice, proposals)
5. ⑤ social relations
6. ⑥ structuring a conversation

Level tags follow the PCIC order. A few items sit on the line between B1 and B2.

### Phase 0: Foundations (done)

P-A1–P-A4 (226 production cards) and Coloquial 1–3 (112 recognition cards). Covers past narration, the future, the conditional and the first si-clauses.

### Phase 1: The office core (B1, months 1–3)

| Layer | Content | Functions |
|---|---|---|
| **W-1 Office basics** ✅ | Perfecto in stand-ups; blockers with *hasta que / en cuanto* + subjunctive; what happened; meetings; coffee | ① ② ④ ⑤ |
| **W-2 Interaction** ✅ | Perfecto vs indefinido; reported speech; disagreeing politely; requests and feedback; steering the conversation | ① ② ④ ⑥ |
| **W-3 Plans and time** | *Voy a*, present and future for plans; *cuando / antes de que* + subjunctive; *para el viernes lo habré terminado*; *llevo dos años…*; rescheduling | ① ④ |
| **S-1 Social life** | *Quedar con*; invitations; *me encantaría, pero…*; health (*me duele, estoy malo*); *me alegro de que, qué pena que, menos mal que* | ③ ⑤ |
| **W-4 Problems and incidents** | Causes and consequences (*como, ya que, por eso, así que*); *se me ha olvidado / se ha caído*; owning mistakes (*ha sido culpa mía, tendría que haber avisado*) | ① ② ⑤ |

**Checkpoint, month 3:** a 2-minute stand-up and a 2-minute "what happened" story, with no perfecto / indefinido mix-ups.

### Phase 2: Confident B1+ (months 4–6)

| Layer | Content | Functions |
|---|---|---|
| **N-1 Storytelling** | All the past tenses inside one story; *al principio, de repente, resulta que, total, que…*; listener reactions (*¡no me digas!*) | ① ⑥ |
| **H-1 Hypotheticals** | *Si tuviera…, si hubiera sabido…, ojalá pudiera…, como si…, de haberlo sabido…* | ② ③ |
| **O-1 Opinions** | *Creo que / no creo que, me parece que, dudo que, puede que*; *a lo mejor* vs *quizás* + subjunctive; current topics | ② |
| **W-5 Feedback and 1:1s** | Praise, soft criticism, career conversations; *me gustaría que…, es importante que…* | ② ④ |

**Checkpoint, month 6:** a speaking mock in the DELE B1 format (a monologue plus a conversation). I'll prepare it from the official model exams.

### Phase 3: B2 at work (months 7–9)

| Layer | Content | Functions |
|---|---|---|
| **W-6 Presenting and data** | *En primer lugar, por un lado / por otro, sin embargo, en resumen*; trends (*ha subido un 10 %, se ha duplicado*) | ① ⑥ |
| **W-7 Negotiating** | *Aunque* + subjunctive; *por mucho que, a no ser que, en caso de que, con tal de que*; offers and counter-offers | ② ④ |
| **W-8 Difficult conversations** | Saying no, escalating, conflict, apologising (*siento que…, lamento que…*); impersonal *se* | ② ③ ④ |
| **W-9 Calls, remote work and admin** | Phone and video calls; holidays, payroll, sick leave; customers | ① ④ ⑤ |

**Checkpoint, month 9:** a recorded 5-minute presentation followed by questions.

### Phase 4: B2 social fluency (months 10–12)

| Layer | Content | Functions |
|---|---|---|
| **S-2 Colloquial Spain, spoken** | The most useful slang turned into production cards (*estar hecho polvo, ir a tope, flipar, qué fuerte, mola*), and when not to use it | ③ ⑤ |
| **S-3 Reacting and empathising** | *¡Qué bien que…!, no me extraña que…, lo siento mucho*; humour and irony | ③ ⑤ |
| **G-1 Your gaps** | Built from your gap log: what you wanted to say this year and couldn't | all |

**Destination check, month 12:** a speaking mock in the DELE B2 format (or the real exam). We also compare your month-12 recording with your month-0 recording.

**Total:** 16 layers × 50 = 800 new production cards (W-1 and W-2 included) + 226 existing ≈ **1,026**.

## 5. How we work (the agreement)

**You**
- **Every day, 10–20 minutes:** groups marked ↻ first; if nothing is due, the next new group.
- **Out loud,** including the follow-up sentence. Known = instant, nothing less.
- **Same day at work:** use 2–3 sentences from today's group.
- **Every week, a gap log:** 5 things you wanted to say and couldn't. Persian, English or broken Spanish are all fine.
- **Every month, a recording:** speak for 3 minutes about your month with your phone's Spanish dictation on, and send me the text.

**Me**
- Build each layer from this roadmap and the card guide (`.kiro/steering/content.md`).
- Turn your gap log into cards, and target any tense pattern I see in your transcripts.
- Update the status table below after each layer, and re-plan with you at each checkpoint.

**When to start the next layer:** when the ↻ list is usually empty and every group of the current layer has been practised at least 3 times. The pace follows you, not the calendar.

## 6. Measuring progress

- **Monthly recording:**
  - accuracy = tense errors per minute;
  - fluency = words per minute.

  These are the standard accuracy and fluency measures in language-learning research (Skehan 1998). We track both in the log below.
- **Checkpoints** at months 3, 6, 9 and 12, as above.
- **In the app:**
  - groups due (↻);

## 7. Pace: what your time buys

This is a simulated year of the app's real review schedule, starting from today's cards. Its assumptions:
- one group takes 6–8 minutes;
- a group still has red cards 80 / 50 / 30 / 20 / 10 % of the time after its 1st / 2nd / 3rd / 4th / later session.

| Daily time | New cards per month | Production cards after 12 months | Out-loud repetitions in the year |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10–12 min | ~45 | ~760 | ~5,400 |
| **15 min** | **~63** | **~990** | **~7,100** |
| 20 min | ~80 | ~1,200 | ~8,700 |

Reviews always come first, so new cards are what's left after keeping the old ones alive. That's why ~1,000 cards fits 15 minutes a day. More time moves the destination closer; it doesn't change the route.

## 8. Decisions to agree

1. **Destination:** B2 speaking in ~12 months. The alternative is B1 in 6 months, then decide again.
2. **Daily time:** about 15 minutes on average.
3. **The two habits:** the weekly gap log and the monthly 3-minute recording.
4. **Layer order:** anything in section 4 to swap, add or drop?
5. **App:** groups you know well come back after 120 days instead of 60. This is already built, and it's what makes ~1,000 cards fit.
